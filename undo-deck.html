One of the most operationally revealing scenarios is lost acknowledgment. The remote system commits the effect, but the network drops the response. The agent sees a timeout and may repeat the operation. In UndoBench, naive retry caused duplicate external effects in 53.33 percent of 960 method-specific trials. This is not saying every retry duplicates an effect, but showing the risk of retrying without effect visibility or server-side deduplication. Source: UndoBench Section 4.2.

UndoBench extends evaluation beyond lost ACK to different phases of execution. Before mutation, safe redispatch is often possible. During a multi-step mutation, intermediate external state may have diverged. After commit but before acknowledgment, reissuing a tool call can duplicate effects. In the four-workflow during-mutation extension, naive retry and standard idempotency both had zero conditional recovery; verify-before-retry recovered some public-state cases, not all. [Dedicated algorithm slide — allow 60–75 seconds; shorten surrounding slides to fit 19 minutes.]

EvoUndo treats recoverability as an admission criterion. A candidate is a tuple ξ=(m,w,u,Cₑ): the forward mutation, a state witness, a recovery specification, and an effect contract. The harness captures relevant pre-state evidence, executes the mutation and typed recovery, and independently verifies restoration and effect coverage over development and held-out IID/OOD states. Only capability-positive candidates meeting recovery admission thresholds are admitted. If recovery fails, the system can improve the witness, typed undo, or effect contract without necessarily rewriting the forward mutation; otherwise it rejects the candidate. This is a simplified algorithm summary, not a formal production guarantee.

Retry, restore, compensate—or stop

RETRY

Only when the operation is known safe to repeat or protected by appropriate idempotency.

RESTORE

Use exact effect-scoped snapshots when state is serializable and directly restorable.

COMPENSATE

For external effects where exact restoration is impossible but a valid compensating action exists.

STOP / ESCALATE

When commit status is unknown and neither observability nor authorized recovery is sufficient.

Effect-scoped snapshots beat EvoUndo in its evaluated in-memory comparison: 300/300 vs 243/300 on different-surface changes.
15 / 17
[16:15–17:45]

The practical lesson is not that every operation needs a generative inverse. Use the simplest correct mechanism. If an operation is idempotent, retry may be appropriate. If a directly restorable effect-scoped snapshot exists, use it; in the in-memory selective-undo comparison, that snapshot baseline outperformed synthesized EvoUndo recovery. If an external operation cannot literally be reversed, use domain-specific compensation. And if the system cannot establish what committed, it should abstain or escalate. Irreversible actions and distributed concurrent side effects remain beyond EvoUndo’s experimental scope.

Three research takeaways. First, UndoBench shows that task competence and recovery capability are distinct. Second, the EvoUndo algorithm captures a state witness, applies a harness mutation, executes a typed recovery plan, and independently verifies that the affected state is restored. It also checks effect contracts and evaluates recoverability across multiple states. Third, only mutations that meet the capability and recoverability admission requirements should be admitted; otherwise repair or reject them.
